    On-floor playbook

    What everyone at the booth says, asks and shows, so a conversation with any account ends in the same place: a scored lead and a dated next step.

    The 60-second pitch

    "Most packers we meet have the same problem: thousands of short runs, constant SKU changes, and temp crews that turn over before they're trained. Fixed automation can't pay back on that work.

    Tutor builds robots that learn a new job fast and work beside your people. You pay by the hour or by the pick, month to month, so there's no capital project. Cassie, here, palletizes and moves cases today. Sonny, our two-armed robot, already picks e-commerce orders and is being trained on pack-out tasks like the ones on your lines.

    What's the job on your floor that's hardest to staff?"

    Pricing claims match Tutor's pricing page: Cassie at $14 to $18 per robot-hour with an 80-hour monthly minimum, per-pick pricing for picking, month-to-month terms.10

    Qualifying questions

    Tutor criterionAsk
    Pooled workHow many brands or programs run through this building in a month?
    Temp labor barHow many of the people on your pack lines are temps today, and how long do they stay?
    Run lengthWhat's a typical run: units, hours, and how many changeovers a shift?
    SKU churnHow often does a new SKU or format show up with less than a week's notice?
    Task fitWhat is the worker doing with their hands: placing, case packing, palletizing? How heavy is each item?
    BuyerWho signs off on a pilot like this: you, the GM, or the owner?

    Demo flow, about eight minutes

    1. Start with their job, not the robot. Ask the hardest-to-staff question and write the answer on the lead card.
    2. Run a Cassie cycle on a mixed case set, then change the case size in front of them to show the changeover.
    3. Show the Sonny video on the booth screen, framed as deployed today in e-commerce fulfillment, with pack-out as its next task. Sonny is billed per successful pick and carries up to 10 lb per arm,1112 so match the example to their items.
    4. Price it on their numbers. Robot-hours times $14 to $18 against their loaded temp cost for the same shift.
    5. Close on a date. Offer two walk-through dates inside 30 days and put one in the tracker before they walk away.

    Objections

    "We tried automation; changeovers killed it."

    That's the problem Tutor is built around. Ask how long their last changeover took and on what equipment, then show the Cassie case-size change. The commitment is month to month,10 so a pilot that doesn't handle their changeovers ends without a stranded asset.

    "We don't have capital budget this year."

    There is no capital purchase. Robots are priced per hour or per pick with month-to-month terms.10 The pricing page lists one-time costs for implementation, a refundable deposit and freight, so be upfront about those.

    "Our customers own the spec; we can't change the line."

    Then bring the brand in. This is why the target list includes brands: a brand that wants faster, steadier pack-out can ask its packer to run a pilot. Offer a three-way call.

    "Our cases are too heavy for that."

    Separate the tasks. Full-case palletizing is Cassie's job. Item placement is Sonny's, at up to 10 lb per arm.12 Note the weights on the lead card instead of guessing.

    "What happens to my people?"

    Lead with the staffing problem they described, not headcount. The robots take the shifts that are hardest to fill, and their trained people run more lines.

    "Who else uses this?"

    Use only customers Tutor has named in public, such as Y International in Lyndhurst, NJ, which Packaging World profiled in 2024 running high-changeover packaging on Tutor's usage-based model (confirm it is still active before naming it).13 Offer a reference call only with the customer's permission.

    ProMat 2027: the second stop

    ProMat runs April 19 to 21, 2027 at McCormick Place in Chicago, five days after Atlanta. MHI expects more than 50,000 manufacturing and supply chain professionals and more than 1,000 exhibitors.9

    Why go

    ProMat is a material handling and logistics show, so its crowd is warehouse and 3PL operations: the buyers for Cassie's case and pallet work and for Sonny's first job, e-commerce fulfillment.11 It also lands next to the lead list's Chicago cluster.
