Expansion Radar · PACK EXPO Southeast 2027 plan · Carrier partnership brief
Prepared for Tutor Intelligence Abdullah Usman · November 2026

Tutor's next lead engine is already walking its target plants: workers' comp carriers.

Every packing line that still moves cases by hand creates injury claims, and an insurance company pays for them. Those insurers already send safety consultants into the same CPG plants and co-packers Tutor sells to. A referral program with the largest comp carriers could turn those consultants into a steady source of warm introductions to the plants with the most hand lifting, adding up to 90 new sites a year at scale, while Tutor keeps the direct sale. The main cost is one person's time.

The background, in four facts
1
Someone already pays for every lifting injury.
Every employer carries workers' comp. When a packer hurts their back, the carrier pays: $47,316 for an average lost-time claim (NCCI).
2
Lifting is the costliest injury.
Overexertion costs $13.07B a year, the #1 cause (Liberty Mutual), and is 36% of Travelers' manufacturing claims. Case packing and palletizing are exactly this work.
3
Carriers already work to prevent it.
Their loss-control consultants walk policyholders' floors, score risky tasks and recommend fixes. Some already fund safety tech.
4
The risk sits with temp labor, Tutor's competition.
32.2% of temp agency workers are in manufacturing, and staffing firms buy their own comp. Zurich runs a program just for them.
The idea: put Tutor on the consultant's list of fixes
How one referral would flow, start to finish
1. Site visit
A carrier consultant walks a co-packer's floor and flags the hand-lifting stations driving its claims.
→
2. Referral
Tutor is on the consultant's list of recommended fixes for that hazard, with a one-page brief.
→
3. Direct sale
Tutor scopes the station and signs the plant on its own contract and price.
→
4. Lifts removed
Robots take over the station; Tutor reports the lifts removed each month.
→
5. Everyone wins
Fewer claims for the carrier; lower injury costs and premiums for the plant over time.
What each side gets
The carrier
Pays fewer of its most expensive claims, and gets a concrete fix to recommend to clients.
The plant
Removes the stations that hurt people, with no capital purchase (Tutor bills by the hour or the pick), and builds a safer record that can lower its premium.
Tutor
Pre-qualified introductions to the plants with the most hand lifting, from someone they already trust. The cost is one person's time, not booths, ads or sales hires, and Tutor gives up no margin.

Why this isn't a channel partnership

A channel partner resells the robots and takes a cut. Here the carrier never sells, prices or signs anything: it makes an introduction, the way a doctor refers a patient to a physical therapist. Tutor signs the plant directly, on its own terms.
