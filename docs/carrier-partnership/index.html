Expansion Radar · PACK EXPO Southeast 2027 plan · Carrier partnership brief
Prepared for Tutor Intelligence Abdullah Usman · November 2026

Who already pays for the lifting Tutor automates? Workers' comp carriers.

Every packing line that still moves cases by hand creates injury claims, and an insurance company pays for them. Those insurers already send safety consultants into the same CPG plants and co-packers Tutor sells to. A referral program with two or three carriers could turn those consultants into a steady source of warm introductions to the plants with the most hand lifting, while Tutor keeps the direct sale.

The background, in four facts
1
Someone already pays for every lifting injury.
Every U.S. employer carries workers' compensation insurance. When a packer hurts their back lifting cases, the carrier (Liberty Mutual, Travelers, The Hartford and others) pays the medical bills and lost wages. An average lost-time claim runs $47,316 (NCCI).
2
Lifting is the most expensive injury there is.
Insurers call it overexertion: lifting, pushing, carrying. It is the costliest cause of serious U.S. workplace injury at $13.07B a year (Liberty Mutual), and 36% of Travelers' manufacturing claims. Case packing and palletizing, Tutor's core work, are exactly these tasks.
3
Carriers already work to prevent it, inside the plants.
Large carriers run loss-control teams: safety consultants who walk policyholders' floors, score risky tasks and recommend fixes. Liberty Mutual's consultants score lifting risk with their own app. Some carriers already fund safety tech for clients.
4
The risk sits where Tutor's competition sits: temp labor.
Tutor's bar is the cost of a temp crew, and 32.2% of temp agency workers are in manufacturing. Staffing firms buy their own comp coverage; Zurich runs a program just for them. That makes staffing carriers a second door.
The idea: put Tutor on the consultant's list of fixes
How one referral would flow, start to finish
1. Site visit
A carrier consultant walks a co-packer's floor and flags the hand-lifting stations driving its claims.
→
2. Referral
Tutor is on the consultant's list of recommended fixes for that hazard, with a one-page brief.
→
3. Direct sale
Tutor scopes the station and signs the plant on its own contract and price.
→
4. Lifts removed
Robots take over the station; Tutor reports the lifts removed each month.
→
5. Everyone wins
Fewer claims for the carrier; lower injury costs and premiums for the plant over time.
What each side gets
The carrier
Pays fewer of its most expensive claims, and gets a concrete fix to recommend to clients.
The plant
Removes the stations that hurt people, with no capital purchase (Tutor bills by the hour or the pick), and builds a safer record that can lower its premium.
Tutor
Gets pre-qualified introductions to plants with the most hand lifting, from someone the plant already trusts, at no sales cost and no margin given up.

Why this isn't a channel partnership

A channel partner resells the robots and takes a cut. Here the carrier never sells, prices or signs anything: it makes an introduction, the way a doctor refers a patient to a physical therapist. Tutor signs the plant directly, on its own terms.

It has been done with other safety tech
Amerisure piloted HeroWear exosuits with policyholders' workers (2024).
Voxel offers Liberty Mutual clients up to 20% off its safety AI.

Kinetic and Nationwide bundled a wearable free into comp policies from 2021; Kinetic is retiring it in 2026-27, so the program needs a hard number behind it (model C).
